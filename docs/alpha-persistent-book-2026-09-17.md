### What improved and what failed

- **Netting works:** all four SIP hypotheses reduce unnecessary turnover. At primary
  costs, slow momentum falls from 51.86 to 18.90 times initial capital across the two
  annual books. That is a mechanical cost improvement, not new predictive information.
- **Instability dominates:** momentum/residual momentum gain in 2022 and lose in
  2023; reversal and the blend show the opposite net-return pattern. Mean IC is
  near zero or negative. Every reported per-fold HAC interval includes zero.
- **Combination is insufficient:** the SIP persistent blend gains 2.91% overall at
  primary costs but loses 0.36% under stress. Its 2022 primary return is −2.03%;
  the largest positive 20-session block is 148.6% of the total signed log gain.
  IEX gives +2.84%/−0.41%, with 203.2% concentration. Pooling gains hides bad years.
- **Costs shifted:** borrow now matters more than turnover for several books.
  With half the capital short, 1%/3% annual borrow is material even when holdings
  change infrequently. These remain declared assumptions, not measured loan rates.
- **IC and profit can differ:** IC ranks every date/all nine names against a fixed
  20-session label. The book trades only scheduled dates/tails, lets exposure drift
  and has a terminal close. They measure different economic samples. Neither
  positive pooled profit nor a low-variance score blend proves forecasting skill.
- **No detected arithmetic explanation:** independent formula, rank, covariance,
  HAC and Decimal cash-ledger reconstruction agree. The IEX warmup failure is a
  real retained coverage limit, separate from the negative economic results.
