# Pool events, pair controls, ration the trials

*Literature pass, 2026-09-28. Power and hurdle tables are the author's arithmetic from the cited formulas; see the lane contract in [alpha-pooled-mining.md](alpha-pooled-mining.md).*

Quant funds and academic researchers do not get around sparse per-symbol trade counts by extracting more from each symbol. They change the unit of hypothesis to a rule applied across a whole cross-section (an event study, a pooled panel regression, a cross-sectional information coefficient) and then pay two separate costs. The first is for dependence, handled with date clustering, calendar-time portfolios, HAC errors or session-block bootstraps. The second is for search, handled with t > 3 hurdles, a deflated Sharpe ratio charged at an *effective* trial count, FDR or Romano-Wolf control, and a holdout used once. Your two results fit this picture closely. The per-symbol genetic miner cannot find anything by construction: **14 trades give a standard error of about 0.33R**, while a DSR ≥ 0.95 gate charged at 8,879 trials acts like a **t ≈ 5.5 requirement, which works out to roughly 1.8R of average profit per trade**. PEAD passed because it was one pre-registered hypothesis per leg, pooled over 4,726 events and tested against an every-reporter control, and **pairing cut its standard error 2.4-fold**. Pooling still delivers less than the raw count suggests. Back-calculating from PEAD's own interval, its 4,726 trades carry the information of **roughly 600–900 independent trades**, because the number of trading sessions, not the number of names, caps the effective sample. The design this report recommends is a pooled lane in which each formula counts as one trial and is scored across the whole cohort by a *session-paired* bracket-R edge against the eligible universe. That edge is tested with a session-block bootstrap, the search is limited to about 100–200 formulas chosen from priors, and the survivors are confirmed once, with Holm control over at most five candidates, on a purged holdout that no study has read. Calibrated on PEAD's variance, this lane can detect paired edges of **about 0.08–0.10R**. Edges the size of PEAD's 0.055R can only be found as a priori hypotheses, which is the strongest reason to keep growing the catalog alongside the miner.

## Fourteen trades cannot clear a 5.5-sigma bar

Start with the basic sample-size arithmetic. For a one-sample test of mean R per trade, the independent-trade requirement is N = (z₍α/2₎ + z_β)²·(σ/δ)². At two-sided 5% significance and 80% power the constant is about 7.85 ([NCSS](https://www.ncss.com/wp-content/themes/ncss/pdf/Procedures/PASS/Two-Sample_T-Tests_using_Effect_Size.pdf); [Real Statistics](https://real-statistics.com/students-t-distribution/statistical-power-of-the-t-tests/)). With a per-trade standard deviation of 1.2–1.8R and edges of 0.05–0.15R, the requirement runs from about 500 to more than 10,000 *independent* trades:

| Per-trade σ | δ = 0.05R | δ = 0.10R | δ = 0.15R |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2R | ≈4,520 | ≈1,130 | ≈500 |
| 1.5R | ≈7,065 | ≈1,770 | ≈785 |
| 1.8R | ≈10,170 | ≈2,545 | ≈1,130 |

Set against that table, a validation window with **14 trades has a standard error near 0.33R** at σ ≈ 1.25R, so even a strong 0.15R edge shows up at t ≈ 0.45. Nothing in the per-symbol funnel's search design can fix this. The same formula run across 150 names would see about 2,100 trades per window instead of 14, and that single change is what separates the PEAD study from the miner.

The multiple-testing gate makes the problem worse. The deflated Sharpe ratio moves the null benchmark from zero to the *expected maximum* Sharpe ratio that N skill-free trials would produce. That benchmark is roughly √V·[(1−γ)Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γZ⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))], where V is the cross-trial variance of Sharpe estimates and γ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. It rises with both N and V ([Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio)). The funnel's frozen reference variance is 0.00288, which implies about 350 observations per trial, so the trial Sharpe dispersion is essentially estimation noise ([power diagnosis](alpha-power-diagnosis-2026-09-18.md)). When V ≈ 1/T, "DSR ≥ 0.95" reduces to a t-statistic hurdle of the expected-maximum multiplier plus 1.645. The table below is my own arithmetic from those formulas, with the last column assuming σ ≈ 1.25R:

| Effective trials | E[max] multiplier | DSR ≥ 0.95 as a t-hurdle | Bonferroni one-sided 5% | Mean R/trade needed at 14 trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1.19 | 2.84 | 2.33 | ≈0.95R |
| 16 | 1.80 | 3.45 | 2.73 | ≈1.15R |
| 100 | 2.53 | 4.18 | 3.29 | ≈1.39R |
| 1,000 | 3.26 | 4.90 | 3.89 | ≈1.64R |
| 8,879 | 3.83 | **5.48** | 4.39 | **≈1.83R** |

At 8,879 trials the null benchmark alone is about 0.205 per observation. If those observations are daily, that is roughly 3.3 annualized, before DSR asks for significance above it. The raw ledger count also overstates N. A GP population of mutations and crossovers is full of near-duplicates, and López de Prado's follow-up work frames the fix as estimating the number of *essentially independent* trials by clustering (ONC, hierarchical or spectral methods) ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Data-Science-Solution-to-the-Multiple-Testing-in-Prado/2a56cd3d9b611b320a26980c05f21e4bfaca4484); [SSRN 3193697](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3193697)). Declustering would not rescue a 14-trade window, though. Even at five effective trials, passing takes about 0.95R per trade.

The internal 800-search diagnosis shows that statistics are only one of three failures that occur together. Planted positive signals were accepted **0/64** times. The GTC entry policy left orders pending at nearly every boundary, which **suppressed about 90% of later signal pulses**. Selected winners had negative holdout IC while their bracket returns rode the generator's drift ([power diagnosis](alpha-power-diagnosis-2026-09-18.md)). The academic record shows what happens when search is left uncorrected. Chordia, Goyal and Saretto generated about **2.1 million strategies and only 17 survived** multiple-testing control. They estimated that **45.3%** of naive rejections would be false ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/p-Hacking:-Evidence-from-Two-Million-Trading-Chordia-Goyal/2007552e53b2d874f929e91cb56341e18887e16b); [SSRN 3017677](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3017677)). Harvey, Liu and Zhu catalogued 316 published factors and concluded that a new factor needs **t > 3.0**, and that the bar keeps rising as more factors are tested ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592); [Foxholm summary](https://foxholm.com/q/research/harvey-liu-zhu-cross-section/)).

## Pooling multiplies trades, but trading days cap the information

Event studies exist because they add events without needing more calendar history. That gain lasts only while the events are independent. Kothari and Warner note that cross-correlation of abnormal returns is negligible for short windows that do not cluster in calendar time. It becomes material when events cluster or horizons are long, and a pooled 20-session bracket study has both problems ([Kothari & Warner](https://www.jufinance.com/mag/dba_19/event_study_chapter_1_2008_vol_1.pdf)). Cluster-sampling theory expresses the cost as a design effect, **DEFF = 1 + (m−1)ρ**, where m is events per cluster (here, per session) and ρ is their intra-session correlation. An ICC of 0.05 with 50 per cluster already triples the required sample ([PracTools vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/PracTools/vignettes/Design-effects.html)). Widening the universe raises m and therefore DEFF, which cancels much of the apparent gain. As m approaches the whole cross-section on a shared macro or earnings day, the effective sample approaches the **number of independent sessions**.

The PEAD study measures this directly. Its long leg reported +0.149R with a 90% interval of [+0.070, +0.232]. Its intervals came from a stationary bootstrap over whole decision sessions (mean block length 10), and its 4,726 trades fell on 1,275 sessions, a median of 2 and a p90 of 9 per session ([PEAD result](apriori-pead-2026-09-26.md)). The interval implies a standard error of about 0.049R. Independent trades with σ = 1.2–1.5R would give 0.017–0.022R. The implied **DEFF is about 5–8 and the effective sample is about 590–930 trades**, which is below the number of sessions. This is my back-calculation, and it assumes the unreported per-trade σ is in that range. Its exit mix of 40% stops, 5% targets and 55% time exits is consistent with σ near 1.25R.

The same study shows the most effective remedy. The paired edge over the every-reporter control, +0.055R with interval [+0.021, +0.089], has a **standard error of about 0.021R, 2.4 times smaller than the leg's**. Differencing within sessions removes the shared market shock, cutting variance by a factor of about 5.7. The paired edge is only about a third the size of the leg's mean, yet its t-statistic (≈2.66) is close to the leg's (≈3.03). The control also corrects the *point estimate*. Every-reporter longs earned +0.094R from 2016–2026 market drift and survivorship, so only 37% of the leg's mean was signal. Clustered errors fix the standard error, and a matched control fixes the bias. A pooled lane needs both, and neither can stand in for the other.

For the standard error itself, finance offers two well-vetted routes. The first is two-way clustering by date and by symbol on the trade panel. Thompson shows the covariance equals the firm-clustered plus the time-clustered estimate minus the White estimate, and that clustering on only one dimension is invalid when residuals correlate on both ([Thompson 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X10001923); [Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1198/jbes.2010.07136); [Petersen 2009](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/22/1/435/1585940)). The second is the calendar-time portfolio: collapse each session's open positions into one return and apply Newey-West with a lag near the holding length ([Fama 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X98000269)). Mitchell and Stafford showed that event-firm returns are **positively cross-correlated when they overlap in calendar time**, and that correcting for this shrinks apparent long-run abnormal performance substantially ([Mitchell & Stafford 2000](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/209645)). Plain Fama-MacBeth handles cross-sectional correlation but not the serial correlation from overlapping 20-session holds ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fama%E2%80%93MacBeth_regression)). Newey-West also fixes inflated t-statistics without fixing overlap-induced point bias ([SSRN 7022678](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7022678)). Bracket payoffs are skewed, bounded near −1R with upside to +3R. Lyon, Barber and Tsai found skewness distorts ordinary t-tests on long-horizon returns and that bootstrap tests are more powerful ([LBT 1999](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~eofek/PhD/papers/LBT_Improved_JF.pdf)). That makes a **stationary bootstrap over whole sessions** ([Politis–Romano via SAS](https://blogs.sas.com/content/iml/2021/01/20/stationary-bootstrap-sas.html)) the right primary test, with the other two as cross-checks. When they disagree, clustering is probably overstating the result.

Cross-sectional IC evaluation runs into the same limit on the time axis. The standard test is **t = ICIR × √T**. For example, a mean IC of 0.05 with a per-period SD of 0.10 over 20 periods gives t = 2.24, and when holding periods exceed the observation spacing the naive t overstates significance ([QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/articles/diagnostics-information-coefficient)). A 20-session horizon gives only about 12.6 non-overlapping cross-sections per year. Reaching t = 2 then needs an ICIR of about **0.56 with one year of data, 0.33 with three and 0.25 with five**; t = 3 over five years needs about 0.38. The Grinold–Kahn breadth term is inflated for the same reason: an S&P 500 universe is not 500 independent bets ([ReSolve](https://investresolve.com/the-case-for-tactical-alpha-part-2-the-fundamental-flaw-of-grinolds-fundamental-law/)). De Prado's uniqueness weights, purging and embargo address a related but separate problem. They keep overlapping labels from leaking or being over-weighted when *fitting* a model ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/19850); [purged CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). They are the right hygiene for training, not a substitute for a dependence-robust standard error.

## Industrial miners score one formula across the whole cross-section

Published formulaic-alpha practice does not treat a formula-by-symbol pair as the unit of evaluation. Kakushadze's **101 Formulaic Alphas** are built from cross-sectional operators (`rank`, `scale`, `indneutralize`) and time-series operators. They have average holding periods of 0.6–6.4 days and an average pairwise correlation of **15.9%** ([arXiv 1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00991); [operator overview](https://coriva.eu.org/en/alpha101-overview/)). Community documentation of WorldQuant BRAIN describes a composite fitness of **√(|returns| / max(turnover, 0.125)) × Sharpe**, a pass bar above 1.0 and Sharpe above about 1.25 ([community docs](https://github.com/alexisdpc/WorldQuant-alpha-trading/blob/main/ImprovingAlphas.md)). Submissions must also pass sub-universe stability, weight concentration and **self-correlation of PnL against the existing alpha library** ([DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/xiegengcai/world-quant-brain/4.1-self-correlation-analysis)), with delay-1 data as standard hygiene. These BRAIN details come from third-party sources, not WorldQuant's official specification.

Microsoft's Qlib separates signal quality (IC, ICIR, Rank IC, Rank ICIR) from portfolio results. It reports each benchmark as a mean over **20 random seeds** ([Qlib benchmarks](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/qlib/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)). Its Alpha158 label skips a bar, `Ref($close,-2)/Ref($close,-1)-1`, so that the scored return starts when a trade is actually possible ([issue #1514](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/issues/1514)). It converts scores into positions with **TopkDropout**: hold K names (for example 50) and swap only the N worst (for example 5) each day, which bounds turnover and rank churn ([strategy docs](https://qlib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/component/strategy.html)). The recent academic miners go further and optimize at the level of a *pool* of alphas. AlphaGen rewards a candidate by its contribution to a combined pool, using a 20-day forward-return target on A-shares ([GitHub](https://github.com/RL-MLDM/alphagen)). AlphaForge accepts a factor only if it clears a correlation threshold against every accepted factor, and retrains annually on a rolling walk-forward schedule across 2018–2022 test years ([arXiv 2406.18394](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18394v2)). AlphaAgent penalizes AST similarity to known alphas, misalignment with a stated hypothesis, and complexity, and uses a static split with training 2015–2019, validation 2020 and testing 2021–2024 ([arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2)).

The two numbers in that literature most relevant to your system are both sobering. AlphaAgent's out-of-sample IC was **0.0056 on the S&P 500 against 0.0212 on CSI 500**, with information ratios of 1.05 and 1.49, so A-share magnitudes do not carry over to US large caps ([arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2)). Plain GP search is also known for "sporadic effective alphas" in a very large search space ([arXiv 2412.00896](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00896v1)). None of these frameworks evaluates fixed-bracket discrete trades. All of them score continuous forward returns in rebalanced books, and all hold out *calendar intervals across the whole cross-section*, never symbols. To translate an IC into your units, I use a rough Grinold-style approximation (not a sourced result): a top-k paired edge is about IC × cross-sectional R dispersion × the average z-score of the chosen names. For the top 2% of 150 names (z̄ ≈ 2.4) at σ ≈ 1.25R, that gives **δ ≈ 3 × IC**. An IC of 0.03 at the bracket horizon maps to about 0.09R. An AlphaAgent-like 0.0056 maps to about 0.02R, and the horizons differ, which is well below anything detectable.

## Multiple-testing control belongs where selection touches the data

The control methods divide by the question they answer. **White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA** test whether the *best* of many rules beats a benchmark once the whole search is accounted for. SPA is more powerful in most settings. **Romano-Wolf stepdown** identifies *which* rules survive at a controlled family-wise error rate, using one shared stationary bootstrap, so adding more formulas costs little extra computation ([SSRN 685361](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=685361); [step-SPA](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~ckuan/pdf/Step-SPA-20090720.pdf)). **Barras, Scaillet and Wermers** control the false-discovery *proportion*. They estimated that 76.6% of mutual funds have zero alpha, 21.3% negative and 2.1% positive ([SSRN 869748](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=869748)), though later work argues the method is conservative ([Andrikogiannopoulou & Papakonstantinou](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12784)). **PBO via CSCV** estimates how often the in-sample winner ranks below the median out of sample, using only a periods-by-configurations return matrix, which a pooled lane produces naturally ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). Harvey and Liu stress that the haircut is **nonlinear**: top Sharpe ratios are penalized modestly and marginal ones heavily, so a flat 50% discount is wrong ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting); [SSRN 2345489](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2345489)).

The serious counter-view comes from Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen. They fit a hierarchical Bayesian model to 153 characteristics grouped into 13 themes across 93 countries and found that most factors replicate. In their model, evidence *grows stronger* as more related factors are tested, because each factor is shrunk toward its theme's mean ([NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432); [Journal of Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13249)). This does not argue for dropping correction. It argues that structure, meaning families of related hypotheses, can take the place of blunt Bonferroni penalties.

The most useful practical principle is that the multiple-testing penalty applies *where selection and testing share data*. Suppose k candidates are frozen in advance and tested once on data that played no part in selecting them. Bonferroni over k then controls the family-wise error of the confirmed claims *by itself*. For k = 5 at 5%, the bar is **p ≤ 0.01, or t ≥ 2.326 one-sided**. Holm relaxes the later steps to 0.0125, 0.0167, 0.025 and 0.05 (standard Bonferroni and Holm logic, as described in [HLZ](https://foxholm.com/q/research/harvey-liu-zhu-cross-section/)). The discovery-stage correction, for example |t| ≥ 3.89 for Bonferroni over 500 formulas, then has a different job: it keeps the one-shot holdout from being spent on noise. A more conservative reading requires both stages to hold. Strictly, the confirmation stage is sufficient for the claims, provided its data are truly untouched. The discovery stage is the right place to be efficient.

For your system, this means the lifetime per-symbol family count of 8,879 does not need to be imported into a pooled lane's confirmation test. The lane still needs its own ledger that charges every trial it evaluates, and it must never reuse its confirmation window. That would be a new pre-registered policy for a new lane, not a relaxation of the existing gates, and the operator would need to review it as such. Because a historical holdout can be spent only once, later campaigns must either divide the remaining history or rely on prospective evidence. Adapting clinical-trial alpha-spending across campaigns is a reasonable idea, but I found no documented use of it in quant research.

## Shrinkage, meta-labels and synthetic paths reallocate information rather than create it

Hierarchical shrinkage is the most rigorous way to borrow strength across securities. The James–Stein theorem guarantees lower total squared error than unpooled estimates, and sparse units move furthest toward the pooled mean ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/blogs/partial-pooling-explained-intuitively/); [Duke notes](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~pdh10/Teaching/732/Notes/shrinkage.pdf)). Jones and Shanken showed that without shrinkage the maximum posterior fund alpha grows without bound as the number of funds grows, simply from luck ([JFE 2005](http://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Christopher-Jones/pdf/jones_shanken_2005_jfe.pdf)). The limitation matters here. With about 14 trades per symbol, shrinkage pulls nearly every per-symbol estimate to the group mean. That usefully stops the system from promoting a lucky symbol, but it cannot certify real per-symbol edges. Its proper roles are ranking formulas within families (a family → formula hierarchy like JKP's themes) and suppressing symbol noise inside a pooled estimate.

**Meta-labeling** trains a secondary classifier to decide whether a primary signal's trade will reach its target before its stop, trading some recall for precision ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling); [Joubert 2022](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4032018)). Hudson & Thames report gains when it is combined with triple-barrier labels ([PDF](https://hudsonthames.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Does-Meta-Labeling-Add-to-Signal-Efficacy.pdf)). Critics respond that a meta-model cannot find information the primary lacked. In one grid-search experiment it lowered average Sharpe and widened variance, and it needs its own leakage-free data ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14706/why-meta-labeling-is-not-a-silver-bullet/)): "a precision filter, not alpha" ([Substack](https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/meta-labeling-the-technique-that)). Your DSL rules are the kind of simple primary where it works best, but only on a pooled event set such as PEAD's 4,726 events, never per symbol.

**Synthetic and bootstrapped paths** are sound tools for *calibration*: stress tests, overfitting checks and CPCV ([AFML notes](https://reasonabledeviations.com/notes/adv_fin_ml/)). They are not a source of power. Valid inference on synthetic data *loses* power relative to the real data it was built from ([arXiv 2509.20345](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20345)). Generators such as Quant GANs reproduce stylized facts like volatility clustering ([arXiv 1907.06673](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06673)), but a generator that "contains" an unknown small edge is circular. The best use in your pipeline is the one already built, a planted-signal power study, applied to the pooled design.

**Cross-asset transfer** works mainly because pooling makes a flexible model estimable at all. Gu, Kelly and Xiu train one model on the full US stock panel ([RFS 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276)), and global forecasters like DeepAR learn from every series together ([summary](https://medium.com/@sajanit95/scalable-time-series-forecasting-with-deepar-4bd5cd2ef5a3)). For a rule-based DSL, the equivalent is one pooled rule with stratum intercepts for sector, liquidity tier and ETF versus stock, not per-symbol parameters.

**Event-count pooling** points to the OHLCV anomalies with the deepest replication record. The 52-week-high strategy earned about twice the return of ordinary momentum and did not reverse ([George & Hwang 2004](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x)). The MAX effect survives controls for size, value, momentum, reversal and illiquidity ([Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rwhitela/papers/max%20jfe.pdf)). Pre-FOMC drift is an index-level anomaly with about 8 events a year, and it has weakened since press conferences began in 2016 ([Quantseeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift)). Every published magnitude needs a discount. McLean and Pontiff found returns across 97 predictors **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([Journal of Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)).

## A pooled lane sized to detect a 0.09R paired edge

### One formula, one trial, one session-paired series

Treat each formula as a single hypothesis evaluated across the frozen cohort. That means 150–300 equities, eligible on each session only through the point-in-time liquidity rule PEAD used: median 20-session dollar volume at least the static universe's 25th percentile, and a close of at least $10, both measured as of the decision ([PEAD result](apriori-pead-2026-09-26.md)). Compute scores at the close of day D, using only data available then (delay-1). Rank them cross-sectionally, demeaned by sector or taken as residuals against SPY or the sector ETF. Take the **top k = 3 names per session** above a minimum score. Enter with session-bounded orders, the version-5 resting lifetime, because GTC entries destroyed conversion in the power study. Label every candidate with the existing conservative bracket labeller (2×ATR stop, 3R target, 20-session time exit), so that mining and the cards score the same payoff ([pipeline](alpha-pipeline.md)).

The decision statistic is the **session-paired edge**. For each session, d_t is the mean R of the chosen names minus the mean R of *every eligible name* run through the same bracket that session, and the lane tests the mean of d_t. Its standard error comes from a stationary bootstrap over sessions with a mean block length of at least 20 to match the hold length. Two cross-checks are required: a calendar-time Newey-West test with lag 19, and two-way date × symbol clustering at the trade level. Report DEFF and effective N alongside every result. Rank IC against the 20-session residual return, with HAC errors, serves only as a cheap pre-screen. Fixing k bounds same-day clustering at DEFF ≤ 1 + (k−1)ρ. Using three names rather than the one or two a card shows buys power while staying close to what the cards trade.

### The arithmetic sets a 0.09R target and a 200-trial budget

PEAD provides the variance calibration. A paired standard error of 0.0207R over 1,275 sessions implies a **per-session paired SD of about 0.74R**, already inflated for autocorrelation between overlapping holds. The event sessions needed for 80% power are then ((t* + 0.84) × 0.74 / δ)². This is my arithmetic. Years assume a formula that fires on all 252 sessions:

| Decision threshold t* | δ = 0.05R | δ = 0.10R | δ = 0.15R |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.645 (single a priori hypothesis) | 1,347 (5.3 y) | 337 (1.3 y) | 150 (0.6 y) |
| 2.326 (Bonferroni over k = 5 confirmations) | 2,186 (8.7 y) | 547 (2.2 y) | 243 (1.0 y) |
| 3.0 (HLZ hurdle) | 3,215 (12.8 y) | 804 (3.2 y) | 357 (1.4 y) |
| 4.18 (DSR, 100 effective trials) | 5,494 (21.8 y) | 1,373 (5.5 y) | 610 (2.4 y) |

The first row reproduces PEAD: an edge of about 0.055R over 1,275 sessions gives t ≈ 2.66, which serves as a check on the calibration. With a 5.4-year discovery window (≈1,260 sessions), the smallest detectable edge is **0.080R at t = 3 and 0.104R at the DSR hurdle for 100 effective trials**. A 2.6-year confirmation window (≈650 sessions) detects **0.092R** at t = 2.326. A PEAD-sized 0.055R edge would pass that confirmation only about a third of the time. The lane should therefore aim for paired edges of **about 0.09–0.10R** and cap each campaign at **about 200 raw formulas**. Those formulas should come from 10–15 prior-driven families, with PnL clustering used to estimate the effective count. GP search is allowed inside that budget, and every expression it evaluates is charged. Its fitness should be the shrunk paired edge, with a complexity penalty and a correlation cap against PEAD, the native strategies and already-accepted formulas, following AlphaForge, AlphaAgent and BRAIN self-correlation practice. The 0.74R figure is specific to PEAD. A technical formula that fires every day will have longer overlap and possibly heavier same-day clustering, so this SD must be measured for each formula, not assumed.

### A three-window calendar with purges and a single-use holdout

| Stage | Window (≈ PEAD data span) | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | 2016-08 → 2021-12, four expanding folds, 20-session purge + 5-session embargo | Paired edge t ≥ 3 by session bootstrap; same sign in ≥ 3 of 4 folds; PBO (CSCV) under a pre-registered ceiling; empirical-Bayes rank within family |
| Selection | 2022-01 → 2023-12, read once | Freeze top k ≤ 5; same sign and at least half the discovery magnitude |
| Confirmation | 2024-01 → 2026-07, journaled as consumed for the whole cohort before computing | Holm at one-sided 5% (first step t ≥ 2.326); lower 90% bound > 0 on both the leg mean and the paired edge; trimmed and recent-window means positive; non-negative in each liquidity tercile |
| Prospective | Live, capped paper probe | Existing probe caps and −4R kill rule; R realized against the control |

The confirmation criteria mirror PEAD's P1–P4. The cohort's recent years may already have been read by earlier panel and factor studies. The journal's inspected-interval records, not memory, decide whether 2024–2026 is fresh. If it is not, confirmation has to be prospective.

### Five pitfalls that manufacture pooled edge

**Survivorship** is the largest. A current 300-name cohort, like the Nasdaq calendar that drops delisted names, flatters absolute R. Rough estimates put the inflation at 1–2% a year ([HMA Quant](https://hmaquant.substack.com/p/survivorship-bias-and-other-data)), and missing delisting returns bias results upward, with corrections of −30% to −55% ([Shumway 1997](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03818.x)). The paired statistic cancels much of this. It does not cancel all of it, because a momentum-style formula can select *future* survivors, so a point-in-time listing and delisting source should come before any absolute-R claim.

**Look-ahead** creeps in through liquidity screens that use same-day volume ([python.financial](https://python.financial/concepts/look-ahead-bias/)). **Overlapping labels** need purging, a block length at least as long as the hold, and uniqueness weights for any fitted component. **Cross-sectional correlation** needs a fixed k, session pairing and sector-neutral scores. **Corporate actions** and differences in feed conventions (adjusted versus raw prices, IEX versus SIP) create spurious gaps ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/securities/asset-classes/us-equity/corporate-actions)). A formula that spans both feeds must be checked for consistent adjustment.

### Build order: prove power on planted data first

Before any real-data campaign, run the existing power-plan and power-study harness on this design. Plant a 0.10R paired edge in a synthetic panel of 150 names with realistic same-day correlation, and require **at least 80% detection and at most 5% null acceptance**. The 800-search diagnosis showed that zero discoveries says nothing until that check passes. Then run the literature families, such as 52-week-high and MAX/reversal, as a priori catalog entries, because at one trial each they are the cheapest route to power. Run budgeted GP families after those. Add pooled meta-labeling only on top of a confirmed primary signal.

## Conclusion

The resource that limits this lane is calendar time and trials, not names. PEAD's 4,726 trades behave like a few hundred independent observations, and every extra formula searched raises the bar the survivors must clear. Power therefore comes from reducing variance and limiting the search: pairing each session against its own eligible universe cut PEAD's variance nearly sixfold, and a pre-registered hypothesis costs one trial where a GP campaign costs thousands. Widening the universe adds same-day correlation faster than it adds information. The realistic reach of a well-built pooled miner is an edge 1.6–2 times PEAD's paired 0.055R. If US formula ICs resemble AlphaAgent's S&P 500 figure, most mined formulas will fall below that, and the lane should be expected to produce few discoveries.

Two things change in how the funnel should be read. A clean holdout that is used once carries its own error control. That makes lifetime family deflation the right tool for the per-symbol regime, where selection and testing share data, and the wrong tool for a lane with an untouched confirmation window. Second, another zero from the pooled lane would be informative only if the planted-signal check has first shown that the lane can detect a 0.10R edge. Until then, the a priori catalog is the higher-yield source of daily suggestions, and the miner is a slower discovery engine that should be judged on its measured power, not its count of discoveries.
